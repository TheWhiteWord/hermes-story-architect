MESSAGE:

Phase 3 was a one-constant fix, and the brief's own instruction — measure a
30+ scene story before changing it — is what decided it, against the option the
brief offered first.

The finding was reproduced before touching anything: 204px of a 647px pane,
32%, no overflow, exactly as recorded. Then I read the width the panel itself
rendered for 1 to 48 scenes at both panel widths, out of the `svg.jr-svg` it
produced rather than a copy of its rule. That is what settled it. The cap binds
only while the uncapped slot still exceeds 46px — 12 scenes or fewer at 680px,
7 or fewer at 320px. From 20 scenes up the uncapped slot is already below the
cap, so `MAX_SLOT` is inert on long stories. It was never a readability guard;
it only ever bit the short stories where the fill is worst. The widest mark the
ribbon draws per scene is the drives ring at 10.6px across (r 4.3 + a 2px
stroke), so a wide column cannot make a marker unreadable — it only makes it
small relative to its column.
I removed it, and the `Math.max(280, …)` floor on W with it, since the pane is
647px wide and 287px narrowed and neither ever reached 280.

I also checked the one candidate fix that could have broken something: an
aspect cap on the slot. It would have cut the shared axis's act names to
"The…" at 680px, because the axis cuts a name to `floor(bandWidth/6)`. That is
why the answer to 3.3 is "the axis" rather than "the slot rule" — and why the
ribbon does not simply call `valueGeometry`: its 360px W floor would make a
3-scene story at the default panel 360px wide and overflow it, which is the
clipping 3.2 exists to prevent.

Two corrections to the brief: the slot rule is at `value-graph.js:149`, not
`142` (that is inside `valueLabelWidth`), and the finding itself was right.

Verification was DOM assertions, not screenshots — the vision tool was down.
The Chrome probe drives the dashboard's own JS and computes four invariants from
the built DOM: no overflow, every hit column inside the scroll box, no label past
the ribbon's right edge, and no two labels sharing space. The text-vs-text
overlap check is the one that mattered; comparing text against marks gives
false positives, because a label sitting on the mark it labels is normal chart
practice and every text sits inside a transparent hit column.

MESSAGE:

Phase 4 was meant to be the closing phase — put the harnesses somewhere
permanent, sweep for legacy — and the sweep is where the value was. Two readers
of the same rows disagreed, and both were the kind of defect this work exists to
remove: the tab that reads the record and the tab that reads the scene were
telling the user two different things about the same three scenes.

The first: `hasBeats` was `!!sb`. Two of the fixture's three `scene_beats`
records are empty placeholders, so the Journey tab's coverage line said "beat
lists exist for 3" while the Beats tab's own empty state said "Beat list missing"
for two of them. Same rows, opposite answers. `hasBeats` now requires
`beats.length`, and the Beats tab's dot and footer use the same notion, so the
strip cannot warn about a pane that disagrees with it.

The second was found while writing the assertion for the first. `renderSceneBeats`
keyed its empty state on the whole pane — `body || emptyHtml(scene)` — so a
scene with value turns but no beats rendered the header and said nothing at all,
while the strip's dot said "No beat list yet". A dot over a silent pane is worse
than no dot. The empty state now sits exactly where the beat spine would have
gone, so the header still renders and the missing list is still said.

4.1 wrote the inventory before the tests, against the landed files rather than
the plan, and it changed one assignment: the act focus is node, not Chrome. The
fixture has one act and `DASH.loadSampleData()` has none, so there is no chip to
click on any page the repo can build. The render is pure and the click handler is
a three-line delegation to it, so the harness covers the behaviour and a Chrome
check would have needed a fixture that does not exist.

4.2's real finding was that `--dump-dom` makes substring assertions meaningless.
The dumped page carries the whole minified bundle and the injected payload, so
`"sb-gap" in dom`, `"jr-row" in dom` and `"Kael" in dom` are all true whether or
not the tab rendered anything. The two Chrome tests Phase 1 added asserted
exactly those, and would have passed against an empty pane. `_probe` now runs
the dashboard's JS and reads the live DOM into `document.title`, which is also
the only way the four ribbon invariants are measurable. Both weak tests were
rewritten rather than kept alongside stronger ones.

The node harness became `tests/dashboard_tabs_harness.js` and its payload stopped
being a synthetic story: it is what `get_dashboard_data` produced for the repo's
own fixture, so the reader is exercised on the shape that ships. The synthetic
story survives only where the fixture cannot reach. `test_no_bare_inline_handlers`
was carrying a hand-kept copy of `JS_ORDER` that had already dropped six files,
including all four tab and value files — the guard could not see a bare handler
in any of them. It reads `JS_ORDER` now.

The 4.4 sweep removed nothing, which is the honest result: `entity-panels.js`
has no reference to beats at all, so the tabs made no panel code redundant. What
it found was two defects and one decision owed. The decision is recorded rather
than taken: `renderSectionsHtml` renders an unfilled section's placeholder as
content in every entity's Overview, while the Beats tab filters that marker out
of the beat record's notes. That is pre-existing behaviour for every panel, and
changing it is a structural change I am not making unilaterally.

MESSAGE:

The placeholder decision came back as "we don't want placeholders", so the
remaining reader was closed. The finding I recorded as "not changed" was the
largest of them: `renderSectionsHtml` had no filter at all, so every Overview
typeset `[placeholder — scene/Objective]` under a `panel-section-title` as if
the author had written it. Three readers of section bodies could do it, and two
of them each carried their own copy of the filter — the shared renderer, the
Beats tab's notes, and the scene Content read directly in `entity-panels.js`.

The fix is one predicate, `DASH.isPlaceholderSection`, in the sections module
where `renderSectionsHtml` lives, so every reader agrees. The Beats tab's
`notesHtml` now delegates to the renderer instead of keeping its own regex, and
`entity-panels.js` guards the scene Content with the same predicate. The fixture
has placeholders in every entity type, so the Chrome checks cover the scene,
plot and sequence panels and assert the real sections survive alongside.

Writing those checks surfaced a trap in `_probe` itself, which was worth more
than the check. It joined `open_js` and the probe with a newline, so a caller
whose `open_js` did not end in a semicolon produced
`showScenePanel('x')\n(function () {...})()` — one call, applied to the return
value of `showScenePanel`. That throws, the title is never set, and the failure
reads as "the probe never ran", which points at the probe rather than at the
join. The helper now terminates `open_js` itself, so no caller can hit it; the
earlier tab probes only worked because their `open_js` happened to end in `;`.

The probes stay, per instruction.
