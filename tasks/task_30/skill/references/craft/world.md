# World

*The reality the story happens inside: its rules, its period, what it holds
sacred, and who holds power in it.* → `theory/world-and-place.md`

## Requisites

None. A world is created on its own, before anything is placed in it.

## Deciding it

**The name and the one-sentence summary.** The summary is the index label.

- **`rules`** — what is possible in this world, and what is not. These are
  the constraints the story has to obey: a world with a rule in it cannot
  later break it without the break being a decision. One rule per entry, and
  each one a limit or a law rather than a description of the place.
  Everything in the story that is impossible in this world is impossible
  because of what is written here. → `character.md`, `location.md`
- **`period`** — where the world sits in history. A world is a *version* of a
  time, and this is what decides what its people take for granted.
- **`values`** — what this world holds sacred: the concepts of good and evil,
  right and wrong, and the law. The moral, the ethical and the legal are three
  different things and a world can disagree with itself about all three.
- **`power`** — who decides, and how. Not only governments: the family, the
  guild, the employer, whoever is obeyed at a household level. Power is what
  tells a character what they can refuse.
- **The sections** — `Description`, `History`, `Livelihood`, `Power`,
  `Rituals`, `Values`, `Conflict`. The world in prose. `Livelihood` and
  `Rituals` have no field of their own and belong only here: how people earn
  a living and what they customarily do are the world's, not a character's.
  What the world believes and what is actually true are different, and the gap
  between them is where a story gets its pressure — so the description and the
  rules are not the same thing and one does not stand in for the other.

## The couplings

- **A world is written once and read from everywhere.** Nothing repeats its
  rules, values or power onto the characters and locations inside it. A
  character that breaks a world's rule, or a location that a world's power
  structure makes impossible, is a contradiction the world file cannot see. →
  `character.md`, `location.md`

- **A world may have variants**, and a variant is a diff on this one rather
  than a world invented from nothing. A base world has `variant_of` empty.
  → `variant.md`

## Consistency

**Against the project.** The project's `genre` and this world's rules are two
records about the same reality, and nothing compares them. A world of magic
under a genre of sci-fi is one of the two being out of date, and which one is
the user's to say — the agent does not get to decide that the genre was
loose. The project's `setting` and `period` are not a check: they describe the
main story, and a story may hold worlds that are not its main one. → `project.md`
