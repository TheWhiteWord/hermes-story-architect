The main interesting concet in the theory is for me this:

### TECHNIQUE OF SCENE ANALYSIS - Step-by-Step

1. **Step One**: gap
    - Context of Spine
    * Character acts on Scene-Objective under pressure.
    * Unexpected reaction from conflict levels.
    * Gap between expectation and result opens.
2. **Step Two: Note Opening Value:**  Identify value at stake and its positive/negative charge at scene start.
3. **Step Three: Break Scene into Beats:**
    * Analyze first action on text (surface) and subtext (underlying action) levels.
    * Name subtextual action with active gerund phrase (emotive connotations, not literal description).
    * Describe reaction to action with active gerund phrase.
    * Action/reaction exchange = Beat. Beat continues until behavior changes.
4. **Step Four: Note Closing Value & Compare:**
    * Examine value-charged condition at scene end.
    * Compare to opening value.
    * No change = nonevent, flat scene (exposition only).
    * Value change = scene turn.
5. **Step Five: Survey Beats and Locate Turning Point:**
    * Review gerund phrases for character actions.
    * Identify emerging shape/pattern (arc). Even random-seeming behaviors have design.
    * Locate moment of major gap opening between expectation and result. This is the Turning Point.

**Scene Analysis Notes:** Ill-written scenes may lack conflict, be repetitive, have misplaced Turning Points, or lack credibility ("on the nose" dialogue). Analysis helps identify problems and guide rewrites.

### THEORY FOR NEW REDESIGN PROCESS 
THis are sections of the theory that deal with all that may be prerequired, or the process itself of the new entoity.

#### TRUE CHARACTER - Revealed Under Pressure:**

**TRUE CHARACTER Definition:** **Revealed in choices made under pressure. Greater pressure = deeper revelation = truer choice to character's essence.**

**Character Depth:** Beneath surface characterization, what is the core of the person? Loving or cruel?  Known through actions under pressure. "As he chooses, he is."

**Pressure is Essential:** Choices without risk are meaningless.

**Principals in Depth:** Major characters must have hidden dimensions, not be simply what they appear to be. "Principals must be written in depth: they cannot be at heart what they seem to be at face value."

#### AUDIENCE BOND - Action and Reaction

**Focus on the Gap:** Story concentrates on moments where a character's action for a desired reaction instead provokes unexpected antagonism. "The world of the character reacts differently than expected, more powerfully than expected, or both."

#### Concentric Circles of Antagonism - Levels

1. **Inner Conflicts (Innermost Circle):** Mind, body, emotions. Internal struggles.
2. **Personal Conflicts (Middle Circle):** Intimate relationships: family, lovers, friends. Tension between social roles and intimacy.
3. **Extra-personal Conflicts (Outer Circle):** Broader world: social institutions, individuals in society, environment. Societal structures, roles, environment (man-made and natural).

#### Sources of Extra-Personal Conflict - Categories

1. **Social Institutions & Individuals (Institutional Contexts):** Government/Citizen, Church/Worshipper, Corporation/Client.
2. **Individuals (Social/Professional Roles):** Cop/Criminal, Boss/Worker, Customer/Waiter, Doctor/Patient.
3. **Environment (Man-Made & Natural):** Time, space, objects in the environment.

**Framework for Character and Story:** Understanding these levels allows for rich, layered characters and narratives exploring human experience across inner, relational, and wider world conflicts.

#### THE NATURE OF CHOICE - Dilemma as True Choice

**False Choice:** "The choice between good and evil or between right and wrong is no choice at all."  Humans act according to their perceived right/good.

**TRUE CHOICE Definition:** **Dilemma.**

**Dilemma Types:**

1. Choice between irreconcilable goods.
2. Choice between lesser of two evils.

**Dilemma and Character:** Character choice in dilemma powerfully expresses humanity and world.

**Three-Sided Situation:** Genuine choice requires a "three-sided situation," triangular decisions, like life.

#### THE GAP: Subjective vs. Objective Reality

**STORY's Origin:** **"STORY is born in that place where the subjective and objective realms touch."**

**Protagonist's Action and Unexpected Reaction:**

* Protagonist seeks an object of desire.
* Action taken based on *expected* positive reaction from the world.
* *Unexpected* reaction from inner life, relationships, or extra-personal world (or combination) – more powerful or different than anticipated.

**The Gap in Reality:** Objective necessity contradicts subjective probability, creating a "gap" in fictional reality.

**Gap as Collision Point:** Subjective and objective realms collide. Difference between anticipation and actual result.  Discovery of truth in action.

**Character Response to Gap:** Character realizes minimal action is insufficient. Must struggle through the gap, taking a more significant, riskier action.

**Risk and Desire Value:** "The measure of the value of a character's desire is in direct proportion to the risk he's willing to take..." Greater desire value = greater risk.

#### SUBSTANCE AND ENERGY OF STORY - The Gap as Core

**Substance of Story:** The gap between expectation and result. Rift between probability and necessity.

**Building Scenes:** Constantly create breaches in reality (gaps).

#### Characters as Art, Not Humans

**Character Definition (Art):** "A character is a work of Art, a metaphor for human nature."

**TRUE CHARACTER - Choice in Dilemma:**

**TRUE CHARACTER Definition:** **"TRUE CHARACTER can only be expressed through choice in dilemma. How the person chooses to act under pressure is who he is..."** Greater pressure = truer/deeper choice.

**Dimension - Contradiction:**

* **Internal Contradiction:** Within deep character (e.g., guilt-ridden ambition).
* **Characterization vs. Deep Character:** Contradiction between surface and core (e.g., charming thief).
* Contradictions must be consistent.

### THEORY FOR THE DATA THAT CAN BE THAN USED AT CHARATER LEVEL TRACKING

#### THE GAP IN PROGRESSION - Escalating Conflict

**Pattern of Action and Gap:**

1. **Action 1:** Protagonist acts, expects result.
2. **Gap 1:** Unexpected antagonistic reaction, disconfirms reality, increases conflict and risk.
3. **Adjustment:** Protagonist revises reality perception.
4. **Action 2 (Riskier):** Based on revised reality, expecting new result.
5. **Gap 2:**  Again, unexpected reaction, larger gap.
6. **Repeat:**  Pattern continues, escalating risk and action towards final, unimaginable action.

**True Action vs. Mere Activity:**

* **True Action:** Physical, vocal, or mental movement that opens gaps, creates significant change.
* **Mere Activity:** Expected outcome occurs, no or trivial change.

**Gap's Profound Meaning:** Break between expectation and reality is where human spirit and world meet.  World as believed vs. reality as it is.


#### CHARACTER ARC: Change and Transformation

**CHARACTER ARC Definition:** **The finest writing reveals true character and depicts arcs – changes in inner nature – over the course of the story, for better or worse.**

**STRUCTURE FUNCTION AND CHARACTER FUNCTION - Interlocked:**

* **Function of STRUCTURE:** To create progressively increasing pressures, forcing characters into dilemmas and risk-taking choices, revealing their true natures, even unconscious aspects.
* **Function of CHARACTER:** To embody the qualities needed to convincingly enact choices. Credibility is key (age, strength, worldview, etc.).

**Interdependence:** Structure and character are inseparable. Changes in one affect the other.

**Source of Art:** Human need for resolution of stress and discomfort through beauty and harmony, revitalizing life, connecting to truth through sensory experience. Story is primarily "aesthetic emotion": thought and feeling combined.

#### PROGRESSION Types - Increasing Scope and Depth

1. **SOCIAL PROGRESSION:** Widen impact of character actions into society.
2. **PERSONAL PROGRESSION:** Deepen actions into intimate relationships and inner lives.
3. **SYMBOLIC ASCENSION:** Increase symbolic charge of imagery from specific to universal, particular to archetypal. Start with literal images, progress to images with universal meaning.
4. **IRONIC ASCENSION:**  Turn progression on irony. Actions lead to opposite of intended outcome, playing with appearance vs. reality.

**IRONIC ASCENSION EXAMPLES:**

* Gets desired thing, but too late.
* Pushed from goal, but led to it.
* Throws away indispensable thing.
* Steps to goal lead away from it.
* Action to destroy becomes needed for destruction.
* Avoids something miserable, but it's happiness.
